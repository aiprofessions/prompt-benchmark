Your New Diagnosis: Type 2 Diabetes

What this means

Type 2 diabetes means there is too much sugar (glucose) in your blood. Your body is not using insulin well. Insulin is a hormone that moves sugar from your blood into your cells for energy. Over time, high blood sugar can harm your body. The good news: it can be managed.

Your A1c

Your A1c today is 7.4%. A1c is a blood test. It shows your average blood sugar over the past 2 to 3 months.

Your new medicine

Medicine: metformin
Dose: 500 mg, one time each day
When: with your evening meal

Taking it with food helps your stomach. Take it at about the same time every day.

Side effects to expect

Some people get an upset stomach or diarrhea (loose, watery stools). This is common. It usually gets better after a few weeks. Keep taking your medicine unless your care team tells you to stop.

Next steps

   - Follow-up visit in 3 months. You will have a repeat A1c test.
   - Diabetes education class. We have sent a referral. This class teaches you how to eat, stay active, and live well with diabetes.

When to call your care team

   - Stomach upset or diarrhea that is severe or does not get better after a few weeks.
   - You cannot keep food or fluids down.
   - Signs of very low or very high blood sugar: shaking, sweating, confusion, feeling very thirsty, or urinating much more than usual.
   - You miss doses often or are not sure how to take your medicine.
   - Any new symptom that worries you.

If anything in this handout is unclear, ask your care team. This handout is a general guide. It does not replace your clinician's advice.
